# Creator-led resource video

Create a specific, credible vertical video in the creator's own voice. The format combines direct-to-camera presence, a useful on-screen demonstration, and one clear next step. Treat any reference as evidence for broad production patterns—not a source of copy, distinctive edit decisions, or a claim that its creator used AI.

## Workflow

1. **Gather the real offer and audience context.** Identify the creator, product, intended viewer, problem, resource, destination, and desired action. Pull only relevant, authorized personal/brand context. If essential facts or the actual offer are missing, label them as open inputs instead of inventing them.
2. **Choose the format.** For a free tutorial/resource: creator hook → concrete promise → show one useful step → show a truthful result → CTA. For a giveaway, use the same educational structure only if the user has supplied real prize, eligibility, dates, entry rules, and destination; otherwise make it a free-resource promo and do not call it a giveaway.
3. **Write fresh, speakable copy.** Keep the hook short, explain the payoff in plain language, and make the CTA unambiguous. Use the creator's established voice without borrowing the reference's wording, slogans, distinctive metaphors, or transcript. Keep on-screen text concise and synchronized to the spoken idea.
4. **Build a beat-based shot plan.** For each beat, specify approximate duration, purpose, framing, environment, action, product/screen evidence, caption, audio, and transition intent. Mark durations and boundaries as proposed until reviewed against the actual edit. Favor a few intentional setups over a shot-for-shot trace of another creator's work.
5. **Generate or capture assets with identity controls.** Use the creator's approved likeness and voice assets only within their authorized scope. Keep wardrobe, lighting, framing, and face/voice consistent across shots. Show actual product UI or label mockups as illustrative; never fabricate a result, testimonial, customer, metric, award, giveaway mechanic, or product capability.
6. **Choose quality mode by the task.** Use fast/low-cost previews for script, composition, and timing exploration. Upgrade only selected approved shots to higher fidelity. Do not upscale an unusable face/hand/product performance and call it final; regenerate or replace the shot.
7. **Review the complete cut.** Check identity consistency, lip-sync/speech naturalness, factual claims, readable captions, audio intelligibility, pacing, aspect ratio, crop safety, transitions, and CTA destination. Reject obvious synthetic artifacts or embarrassing delivery. Provide a playable review export and note remaining uncertainty; never claim exact replication or publish-ready status without inspecting the rendered video.

## Deliverable

Return a compact brief with:

- audience, promise, offer, and CTA;
- original script and caption text;
- timestamped beat/shot list with visual and audio direction;
- required likeness, voice, screen, and brand assets;
- fast-preview versus high-quality shot choices;
- claim/rights checks and unresolved inputs;
- review status of the actual rendered export.

Keep source attribution with imported references. Do not redistribute source media unless the user has the rights to do so. A giveaway is a real promotion with terms, not a synonym for a free download.
